=== SLIDE 2 ===


Why Standard Entropy Is Not Enough
Not all uncertainty is equally useful for data selection.


ALEATORIC
Data Uncertainty
Inherent ambiguity or noise in the response itself.
Manifests as variable wording, formatting, or phrasing.
Choosing this leads to training on random noise.

EPISTEMIC
Model Uncertainty
The model truly does not know the concept or reasoning step.
Reflects a genuine lack of mathematical knowledge.
Hypothesis: This is the critical signal for trajectory selection.

High entropy is not always useful if it mostly reflects noise instead of missing knowledge.

Experimental Pipeline


Generate Candidate Data
Teacher model: Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct
12,000 math problems
5 reasoning trajectories per problem
60,000 total candidate trajectories


Score and Select Trajectories
Student model: Qwen2.5-3B
Each trajectory scored with 10 MC-dropout passes
Baseline: total entropy
Proposed: epistemic uncertainty via mutual info
One winner selected per scoring rule


Fine-Tune and Compare
Train two separate LoRA student models
One model uses baseline-selected data
One model uses epistemic-selected data
Evaluate on downstream reasoning benchmarks

=== SLIDE 9 ===


Conclusion and Future Work


Main Takeaway
Epistemic uncertainty slightly outperformed total-entropy selection
The improvement was positive on both MATH-500 and GSM8K
The effect size was small


What This Means
Bayesian uncertainty is a promising direction for trajectory selection
Useful for identifying informative reasoning data
Result is not yet strong enough to be considered definitive


Next Steps
Stronger answer normalization and equivalence checking
Combine with diversity-aware selection
Test larger ablations and selection budgets
Improve uncertainty preservation during distillation

This project should be viewed as a promising proof of concept rather than a final conclusive result.
